1997 to 2003

Network

Wi-Fi cuts the cable

Wi-Fi carries network packets over radio. The standard is called 802.11, and the name Wi-Fi came from the industry group that tested devices against it.

A laptop could now be online anywhere in a building, and soon in cafés, stations and airports.

1997

The first 802.11 standard, at 2 Mbit/s.

1999

802.11b reaches 11 Mbit/s, and the group now called the Wi-Fi Alliance forms to certify devices.

2003

Laptops start to include Wi-Fi as standard.

Radio is shared and noisy, so Wi-Fi spends much of its time on taking turns and resending lost packets. That is why it feels slower than its headline speed, and why a busy café network crawls.
