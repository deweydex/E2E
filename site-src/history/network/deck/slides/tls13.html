Encryption becomes the default

Free certificates from Let’s Encrypt, from December 2015, removed the cost of HTTPS. Browsers then began to warn about every plain HTTP page.

TLS 1.3, in August 2018, cut the handshake from two round trips to one, so encryption barely slows the start of a visit.

2015

Let’s Encrypt opens to the public with free certificates.

2018

TLS 1.3 is published. Chrome marks plain HTTP as “Not secure”.

2025

About 88 per cent of sites send visitors to HTTPS by default.

The 2025 figure comes from W3Techs. Encryption also hides which page on a site someone reads, from anyone on the same network. It does not hide which site they visit.
