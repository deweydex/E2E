Content delivery networks: copies near the reader

The problem

Light in fibre needs at least 80 milliseconds to cross from Dublin to San Francisco and back. A page with many files makes that trip many times.

The fix

A content delivery network keeps copies of a site’s images, styles and scripts on servers in many cities, and answers each visitor from the nearest one. Akamai, founded in 1998, was the first big one.

Dublin visitor → Dublin copy, not California

Distance became a design problem. Bandwidth can grow, but nothing makes light faster.

This is the first big change to the shape of a site: the origin server keeps the data and the pages, and copies of the heavy files live all over the world. Most large sites, and many small ones, now sit behind a CDN.
