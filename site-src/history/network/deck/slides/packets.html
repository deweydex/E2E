Network

The first message, and the idea of a packet

Packet switching

A message is cut into small packets. Each packet carries the address it is going to, and can take any free route there. The receiver puts them back in order.

HELLO → [H][E][L][L][O] → HELLO

The first message

On 29 October 1969 a student at UCLA tried to log in to a computer at Stanford Research Institute over ARPANET. The system crashed after two letters, so the first message was “LO”.

A telephone call holds one line open for the whole conversation. Packets share the lines, so a broken link only means a different route.

Leonard Kleinrock's lab at UCLA sent the message. The network was funded by ARPA, part of the US Department of Defense, and linked four sites, three universities and a research institute, by the end of 1969. Packet switching is why the internet survives broken links: traffic flows round them.
